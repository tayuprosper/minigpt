THE FOX AND THE WELL

A fox was walking through the forest on a hot day. The sun was high and the fox was very thirsty. He came to an old well at the edge of a field. He looked down into the well and saw the cool water far below.
"I will drink," said the fox, and he leaned over the stone wall. But he leaned too far, and he fell into the well.
The water was not deep, so the fox did not drown, but the walls were smooth and he could not climb out. He waited and waited. At last a goat came to the well.
"Is the water good?" asked the goat.
"The water is the best in the land," said the fox. "Come down and drink with me."
The goat jumped into the well and drank. Then the fox climbed onto the back of the goat, and from the back of the goat he jumped out of the well.
"Goodbye, friend," said the fox. "Next time, look before you jump."
The goat stayed in the well until the farmer came and pulled him out with a rope.
Look before you leap.

THE ANT AND THE BIRD

An ant went down to the river to drink. The water was fast and the ant fell in. A bird sat in a tree above the river. She saw the ant in the water, and she dropped a leaf into the river. The ant climbed onto the leaf and floated to the bank.
"Thank you," said the ant. "One day I will help you."
The bird laughed. "How can a small ant help a bird?"
A few days later a hunter came into the forest. He saw the bird in the tree and he lifted his bow. The ant saw the hunter. She ran up his leg and she bit him on the foot. The hunter cried out and his arrow flew into the sky. The bird heard the cry and flew away.
"You see," said the ant, "a small friend can be a great friend."
One good turn deserves another.

THE TORTOISE AND THE HARE

The hare was proud of his long legs. Every day he said to the other animals, "I am the fastest animal in the forest. No one can run as fast as me."
One morning the tortoise said, "I will race you."
The animals laughed. The hare laughed the most. "You? You are the slowest animal in the forest. But I will race you, and I will win."
The fox drew a line in the dust and the race began. The hare ran so fast that soon the tortoise was far behind. The hare looked back and he could not see the tortoise at all.
"I have time to rest," said the hare, and he lay down under a tree. The day was warm, and soon the hare was asleep.
The tortoise walked on. He did not stop. He did not rest. Slowly, slowly, he walked past the sleeping hare.
When the hare woke up, the sun was low in the sky. He ran as fast as he could, but it was too late. The tortoise was already at the line, and all the animals were cheering.
Slow and steady wins the race.

THE CROW AND THE JAR

A crow was flying over a dry field. There had been no rain for many days and the crow was very thirsty. At last she saw a jar on the ground. There was a little water at the bottom of the jar, but the jar was tall and the neck was narrow. The crow put her beak into the jar, but she could not reach the water.
She tried to push the jar over, but it was too heavy. She tried to break the jar, but it was too strong.
Then the crow had an idea. She picked up a small stone and dropped it into the jar. She picked up another stone and dropped it into the jar. One by one, she dropped many stones into the jar. With every stone, the water rose a little higher. At last the water was at the top, and the crow drank until she was no longer thirsty.
Little by little does the trick.

THE LION AND THE MOUSE

A lion was sleeping in the shade of a tree. A little mouse ran across his paw and woke him. The lion caught the mouse in his great paw.
"Please let me go," said the mouse. "If you let me go, one day I will help you."
The lion laughed. "How can a mouse help a lion?" But he was not hungry, so he opened his paw and the mouse ran away.
Some days later the lion was caught in a net. Hunters had tied the net between the trees. The lion pulled and pulled, but he could not get out. He roared, and his roar was heard in every corner of the forest.
The little mouse heard the roar. She ran to the lion and she began to chew the ropes of the net. She chewed and chewed, and at last the lion was free.
"You laughed at me," said the mouse, "but now you see that a mouse can help a lion."
No act of kindness is ever wasted.

THE WIND AND THE SUN

The wind and the sun had an argument. "I am stronger than you," said the wind.
"No, I am stronger," said the sun.
They saw a man walking on the road. He wore a heavy coat. "Let us see who can make that man take off his coat," said the sun. "The one who does it is the stronger."
The wind began. He blew and blew. He blew so hard that the trees bent and the leaves flew through the air. But the harder the wind blew, the tighter the man held his coat.
Then it was the turn of the sun. The sun came out from behind a cloud and shone on the man. The air grew warm. The man opened his coat. The air grew warmer. At last the man took off his coat and sat down in the shade of a tree.
Kindness does more than force.

THE DOG AND THE BONE

A dog had a bone. He was walking home across a bridge over a river. He looked down into the water and he saw another dog with another bone. The bone of the other dog looked bigger than his own.
"I want that bone," said the dog, and he barked at the other dog. When he opened his mouth, his own bone fell into the river and was gone. Then the dog saw that the other dog was only his own face in the water.
He went home with nothing.
Be happy with what you have.

THE TWO FRIENDS AND THE BEAR

Two friends were walking through the forest. They promised each other that if danger came, they would stand together.
Suddenly a bear came out of the trees. The first friend ran to a tree and climbed it quickly. He did not wait for his friend. The second friend could not climb, so he lay down on the ground and held his breath.
The bear came to the man on the ground. It sniffed his face and his ears. The man did not move. At last the bear walked away into the forest.
The first friend climbed down from the tree. "What did the bear say to you?" he asked. "It put its mouth close to your ear."
"The bear told me something wise," said the second friend. "It told me never to walk with a friend who runs away when danger comes."
A true friend is known in times of trouble.

THE FARMER AND HIS SONS

An old farmer was dying. He called his three sons to his bed. "My sons," he said, "there is a treasure hidden in our field. Dig, and you will find it."
When the farmer died, the sons went to the field. They dug the whole field. They dug in the north and in the south. They dug in the east and in the west. They found no gold and no silver.
But the field was so well dug that when they planted their seeds, the harvest was the best they had ever seen. They sold the grain at the market and they became rich.
Then the sons understood. The treasure was the work.
Hard work is a treasure.

THE GOOSE AND THE GOLDEN EGG

A man and his wife had a goose. Every morning the goose laid one egg, and every egg was made of gold. The man sold the eggs at the market, and soon he was rich.
But the man was not happy. "One egg a day is too slow," he said. "The goose must be full of gold. If I open it, I will have all the gold at once."
So the man killed the goose and opened it. But inside there was no gold at all. The goose was just like any other goose. And now there would be no more golden eggs.
Those who want too much lose all.

THE BOY WHO CRIED WOLF

A young boy watched the sheep of his village on the hill. The days were long and he was bored. One day he ran down the hill and shouted, "Wolf! Wolf! A wolf is eating the sheep!"
The people of the village ran up the hill to help him. But there was no wolf. The boy laughed at them.
A week later he did it again. "Wolf! Wolf!" he cried. Again the people ran up the hill, and again there was no wolf. The boy laughed again.
Then one evening a real wolf came out of the forest. The boy ran down the hill and shouted, "Wolf! Wolf! Please help!" But the people of the village did not come. "He is lying again," they said.
The wolf ate many of the sheep, and the boy cried all night.
No one believes a liar, even when he tells the truth.

THE MILLER, HIS SON AND THE DONKEY

A miller and his son were taking their donkey to the market. They walked beside it on the road.
A woman saw them and said, "How foolish! They have a donkey and they walk." So the miller put his son on the donkey.
Soon they met some old men. "Look at that lazy boy," said one. "He rides and his old father walks." So the boy got down and the miller got on the donkey.
Then they met some mothers with their children. "What a cruel father," they said. "He rides and his little son walks." So the miller pulled his son up beside him, and they both rode the donkey.
Then they met a man near the town. "Two people on one small donkey! You will kill it." So the miller and his son tied the legs of the donkey to a pole and carried it on their shoulders.
They came to a bridge, and the people of the town laughed so loudly that the donkey kicked. It broke the rope, fell into the river, and swam away.
"I tried to please everyone," said the miller, "and I pleased no one, and I lost my donkey too."
If you try to please all, you please none.

THE TOWN MOUSE AND THE COUNTRY MOUSE

A country mouse lived in a small hole under a tree. One day his cousin, the town mouse, came to visit. The country mouse gave him the best food he had: seeds, nuts and a little dry bread.
The town mouse ate a little and said, "Cousin, how can you eat this simple food? Come with me to the town and I will show you how to live."
So the two mice went to the town. The house of the town mouse was large and warm. On the table there was cheese, cake, honey and fruit. The country mouse had never seen so much food.
But as they began to eat, the door opened and a woman came in. The mice ran and hid. When she left, they came out again. Then a large cat came into the room, and the mice ran and hid again, shaking with fear.
"Goodbye, cousin," said the country mouse. "You may have cake and honey, but I would rather eat my seeds in peace."
Better a little in safety than much in fear.

THE OAK AND THE REED

A great oak stood beside a river. Next to it grew a thin reed. "You are so weak," said the oak to the reed. "The smallest wind makes you bow your head. I stand tall and strong in every storm."
"I bend, but I do not break," said the reed.
That night a great storm came. The wind blew harder than it had ever blown. The reed bent low to the ground and let the wind pass over it. The oak stood tall and fought the wind. It fought and fought, until at last, with a terrible crack, it fell into the river.
In the morning the sun came out. The reed stood up again, as green as before.
It is better to bend than to break.

THE STAR AND THE LAMP

There was once a lamp in the window of a small house. It burned all night, and it was very proud of its light.
"I am brighter than the stars," said the lamp. "I am brighter than the moon. Look how my light falls on the road."
Then a little wind came through the window and blew the lamp out. The house was dark. Above the house, the stars were still shining, and the moon was still shining, just as they had for a thousand years.
The woman of the house lit the lamp again and said, "Shine, little lamp, but do not forget that you are only a lamp."
Pride comes before a fall.

THE BEE AND THE DOVE

A bee fell into a pond and could not fly out. A dove sat on a branch over the pond. She saw the bee and dropped a small twig into the water. The bee climbed onto the twig and dried its wings in the sun.
Later that day a boy came with a stone to throw at the dove. The bee flew to the boy and stung his hand. The boy dropped the stone, and the dove flew away safely.
Kindness comes back to those who give it.

THE OLD MAN AND THE TREE

An old man was planting a small tree in his garden. A young man walked by and laughed.
"Why do you plant that tree?" he asked. "It will take many years to grow. You will never eat its fruit."
The old man smiled. "When I was a boy, I ate the fruit of trees that other people planted. Now I plant trees for the children who will come after me."
We plant for those who come after us.

THE FROG AND THE OX

A young frog was playing in a field when he saw an ox for the first time. He hopped home to his mother as fast as he could.
"Mother," he said, "I saw a great beast in the field. It was as big as a hill, with horns on its head and a long tail."
The mother frog laughed. "That was only the ox of the farmer. He is not so big. I can make myself as big as him." And she took a deep breath and puffed herself up.
"Was he as big as this?" she asked.
"Much bigger," said the little frog.
She took another breath and puffed herself up again. "Was he as big as this?"
"Much, much bigger," said the little frog.
So she took one more great breath, and puffed and puffed, until with a loud pop she burst.
Do not try to be what you are not.

THE CAT AND THE BELL

The mice in an old house were afraid of the cat. Every night the cat crept along the floor without a sound, and every night she caught another mouse. So the mice held a meeting to decide what to do.
Many plans were made, and many plans were thrown away. At last a young mouse stood up. "I have it," he said. "We will tie a bell around the neck of the cat. When she comes, we will hear the bell, and we will have time to run."
All the mice cheered. What a clever plan! Then an old mouse stood up slowly.
"It is a fine plan," he said. "But tell me, which of us will tie the bell around the neck of the cat?"
No mouse said a word.
It is easy to say what should be done, and hard to do it.

THE SHEPHERD GIRL AND THE RIVER

A shepherd girl lived in a village beside a wide river. Every morning she took her sheep across the bridge to the green hills on the other side, and every evening she brought them home again.
One spring there was a great flood, and the water carried the bridge away. The people of the village said, "The sheep must stay on this side now. The grass here is poor, but what can we do?"
The girl did not answer. Every day she carried stones to the river. She put them in a line across the shallow part of the water, one stone after another. The people laughed at her. "One girl cannot build a bridge," they said.
But the girl went on carrying stones. After many weeks, the stones made a path across the river, and the sheep could walk over it to the green hills. Then the people of the village stopped laughing, and they began to carry stones too, and together they built a new bridge, stronger than the old one.
A long road is walked one step at a time.

THE WOLF IN SHEEP'S CLOTHING

A wolf was hungry, but the shepherd watched his sheep so well that the wolf could never catch one. One day the wolf found the skin of a sheep lying in a field. He put it on over his own grey coat and walked into the middle of the flock.
The sheep did not know him. The shepherd did not know him. That evening the shepherd closed the gate and the wolf was inside, among the sheep.
But that night the shepherd wanted meat for his supper. He went into the dark field, took hold of the biggest sheep he could find, and carried it home. It was the wolf.
Those who wear a false face may be caught by it.

THE HEN AND THE BREAD

A little red hen found some grains of wheat in the yard. "Who will help me plant this wheat?" she asked.
"Not I," said the dog. "Not I," said the cat. "Not I," said the duck.
"Then I will plant it myself," said the hen, and she did.
When the wheat was tall and yellow, she asked, "Who will help me cut the wheat?"
"Not I," said the dog. "Not I," said the cat. "Not I," said the duck. So the hen cut it herself.
"Who will help me carry the wheat to the mill?" "Not I," said all three. So she carried it herself.
"Who will help me bake the bread?" "Not I," said all three. So she baked it herself.
At last the bread was warm and golden, and it smelled very good. "Who will help me eat this bread?" asked the hen.
"I will," said the dog. "I will," said the cat. "I will," said the duck.
"No," said the little red hen. "I planted the wheat, I cut it, I carried it and I baked it. I will eat it with my chicks." And she did.
Those who will not work should not expect to share the harvest.

THE MONKEY AND THE NUTS

A monkey found a jar full of nuts. The neck of the jar was narrow. The monkey put his hand into the jar and took a big handful of nuts. But with his hand full, he could not pull it out of the narrow neck.
He pulled and pulled. He cried and shouted. He did not want to let go of a single nut.
A wise old bird sat on a branch above him. "Let go of half the nuts," she said, "and your hand will come out. Then you can put it in again and take the rest."
The monkey did as she said, and soon he had all the nuts.
Do not try to take everything at once.

THE PEACOCK AND THE CRANE

A peacock walked beside a lake, spreading his beautiful tail in the sun. A plain grey crane stood in the water, looking for fish.
"Look at my feathers," said the peacock. "They are blue and green and gold. I am dressed like a king. And you are dressed in dull grey, like a beggar."
The crane said nothing. She opened her wide grey wings and rose up into the air. She flew high above the lake, higher than the trees, higher than the hills, until she was a small dark spot against the clouds.
"You may be dressed like a king," she called down, "but you walk on the ground like a chicken, while I fly up to the stars."
Fine feathers do not make fine birds.

THE TWO POTS

Two pots were floating down a river. One was made of brass and the other was made of clay. The brass pot said to the clay pot, "Stay close to me, friend, and I will keep you safe."
"Thank you," said the clay pot, "but I would rather stay away from you. If the water pushes us together, you will not be hurt, but I will break into a hundred pieces."
The weak should be careful of the friendship of the strong.

THE STAG AT THE POOL

A stag came to a clear pool to drink. As he bent his head, he saw himself in the water.
"What beautiful horns I have," he said. "They are tall and wide, like the branches of a tree. But my legs are so thin and ugly. I wish I had better legs."
Just then he heard the sound of hunters and their dogs. The stag ran. His thin legs carried him quickly across the open field, and he left the dogs far behind. But then he ran into the forest, and his wide horns were caught in the branches of the trees. He could not pull himself free, and the dogs came closer and closer.
At the last moment he broke free and escaped, but he had learned something. "The legs I hated saved me," he said, "and the horns I loved nearly killed me."
We often value the wrong things.

THE FISHERMAN AND THE LITTLE FISH

A fisherman pulled up his net and found only one little fish inside.
"Please let me go," said the little fish. "I am too small to make you a good meal. Put me back in the river, and when I am big and fat, you can catch me again."
The fisherman shook his head. "A small fish in my hand is better than a big fish in the river. If I let you go, I may never see you again." And he put the little fish in his basket.
A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush.

THE GIRL AND THE MILK

A girl was walking to the market with a pail of milk on her head. As she walked, she began to make plans.
"With the money from this milk, I will buy some eggs," she said. "The eggs will become chickens. I will sell the chickens and buy a new dress. When I wear my new dress to the fair, all the young men will want to dance with me. But I will toss my head and say no to them all."
And as she said this, she tossed her head. The pail fell to the ground, and all the milk ran into the dust. No eggs, no chickens, no dress, and no dance.
Do not count your chickens before they hatch.

THE BUNDLE OF STICKS

An old man had seven sons, and they were always fighting with one another. One day he called them together and gave them a bundle of seven sticks tied with a cord.
"Break this bundle," he said to the eldest son. The son pushed and pulled with all his strength, but he could not break it. Each of the other sons tried, and none of them could break it.
Then the old man untied the cord and gave each son a single stick. "Now break your stick," he said. Each son broke his stick easily.
"You see, my sons," said the old man. "If you stand together, no one can break you. If you stand alone, anyone can."
In unity there is strength.

THE MOUSE WHO WANTED A WIFE

A mouse wanted to marry the strongest being in the world. First he went to the sun. "You are the strongest," he said. "Will you marry me?"
"I am not the strongest," said the sun. "The cloud is stronger than me. It can cover my face."
So the mouse went to the cloud. "No," said the cloud, "the wind is stronger. It pushes me across the sky."
So the mouse went to the wind. "No," said the wind, "the wall is stronger. I blow and blow, but the wall does not move."
So the mouse went to the wall. "No," said the wall, "the mouse is stronger than me. Mice dig holes under me, and one day I will fall."
So the mouse went home and married a pretty young mouse from his own village, and they were very happy.
What you are looking for may be close to home.

THE LAZY STUDENT

There was once a student who did not like to study. Every evening, when the other students opened their books, he went to sleep. "I will study tomorrow," he said. "There is plenty of time."
The days went by, and the weeks, and the months. Each day he said, "Tomorrow," and each day he did nothing. At last the day of the great examination came. The other students wrote and wrote, but the lazy student sat with an empty page in front of him.
When the teacher took his paper, she said only this: "Tomorrow is the busiest day of the week, because that is where all the work of today is sent."
Do not leave for tomorrow what you can do today.

THE ELEPHANT AND THE ROPE

A man walking past a camp saw a great elephant standing quietly beside a tree. A thin rope was tied around one of its legs. The rope was not tied to anything strong. The elephant could have broken it with one step.
"Why does the elephant not run away?" asked the man.
The keeper smiled. "When it was very young, we tied it with the same rope. Then it was too small to break it. It pulled and pulled and could not get free. Now it is big and strong, but it still believes the rope can hold it, so it never tries."
Do not let old failures decide what you can do now.

THE BAKER AND THE COINS

A rich man in a hungry town came to the baker and said, "Every morning, put a loaf of bread for each poor child on the table outside your shop. I will pay for them all."
The next morning the children ran to the table. They pushed and shouted and fought to get the biggest loaves. They did not say thank you. But one small girl waited until all the others had gone, and then she took the smallest loaf that was left, and she bowed to the baker.
The next day the same thing happened. The children fought, and the girl waited and took the smallest loaf.
On the third day, when the girl broke her small loaf in half at home, a silver coin fell out of it. She ran back to the baker to return it. "No," said the baker, "the rich man asked me to put it there. It is a gift for the child who waits."
Patience and good manners bring their own reward.

THE CAMEL AND THE TENT

On a cold night in the desert, a man was sleeping in his tent. His camel stood outside in the wind. The camel put his nose into the tent.
"Master," he said, "it is so cold. Let me keep my nose in the tent." The man said yes.
After a while the camel said, "Master, let me put my head in the tent." The man said yes.
Then the camel asked to put his neck in the tent, and then his front legs, and the man said yes each time. At last the camel walked right into the tent, and there was no room for the man at all, so he had to sleep outside in the cold.
Give a little to the wrong person, and they will take it all.

THE OWL AND THE GRASSHOPPER

An owl liked to sleep in the day and hunt at night. But every afternoon a grasshopper sat in the grass below her tree and sang as loudly as he could.
"Please be quiet," said the owl. "I am trying to sleep."
"The day belongs to everyone," said the grasshopper, and he sang louder.
The owl was clever. "Very well," she said. "Your singing is so fine that it has made me thirsty. I have some sweet water in my tree that the gods themselves drink. Come up and share it with me."
The grasshopper was proud and greedy. He hopped up into the tree, and the owl ate him.
Pride and greed lead to danger.

THE GARDENER AND THE PUMPKIN

A gardener was resting under an oak tree. He looked at the tiny acorns on the branches above him, and then at the great pumpkins growing in his garden.
"The world is not well made," he said. "A huge tree has such small fruit, and a small plant has such huge fruit. If I had made the world, I would have put the pumpkins on the oak."
Just then a small acorn fell from the branch and hit him on the nose.
He rubbed his nose and laughed. "If that had been a pumpkin," he said, "I would not have a nose at all. Perhaps the world is better made than I thought."
Things are often as they are for a good reason.

THE STONE SOUP

A traveller came to a poor village late in the evening. He was hungry, but the people of the village said, "We have no food to give you. We have barely enough for ourselves."
"Never mind," said the traveller. "I will make stone soup for us all." He put a large pot of water on a fire in the middle of the village and dropped a smooth stone into it.
The people came to watch. "Stone soup is good," said the traveller, tasting it, "but it would be better with an onion."
A woman went home and came back with an onion. "Very good," said the traveller. "Now if only we had a carrot." A boy ran home and came back with two carrots. Then someone brought some beans, someone brought salt, someone brought a little meat, and someone brought bread.
That night the whole village sat around the fire and ate the best soup they had ever tasted.
When everyone gives a little, there is enough for all.

THE FOX AND THE GRAPES

A hungry fox was walking through an orchard when he saw a bunch of beautiful, dark purple grapes hanging from a high branch. His mouth watered at the sight. "Just the thing to quench my thirst," he said. He took a few steps back, ran, and jumped, but he could not reach them. He tried again and again, but the grapes were always just out of his reach. At last, tired and out of breath, he gave up. As he walked away, he turned his nose up and said, "I am sure they are sour anyway."
It is easy to despise what you cannot have.

THE WOLF AND THE CRANE

A wolf was eating his dinner too fast and a bone got stuck in his throat. He choked and coughed, but the bone would not move. He ran through the forest, offering a great reward to anyone who could pull it out. A crane heard him and agreed to help. The crane put her long neck into the wolf's mouth, took hold of the bone with her beak, and pulled it out safely. "Now, where is my reward?" asked the crane. The wolf showed his teeth and growled, "Be thankful that you put your head into a wolf's mouth and took it out safely. That is reward enough."
Expect no reward for serving the wicked.

THE FOX AND THE STORK

The fox invited the stork to his house for dinner. The fox wanted to play a trick, so he served a thin soup in a very flat dish. The fox lapped it up easily, but the stork could only wet the end of her long bill and went home hungry. "I am sorry you did not like the food," smiled the fox. A few days later, the stork invited the fox to dinner. She served delicious meat, but she served it in a tall jar with a narrow neck. The stork ate easily with her long bill, but the fox could only lick the outside of the jar.
Do not play tricks on others if you cannot accept them yourself.

THE DOG IN THE MANGER

A dog was sleeping in a manger full of hay in a barn. A tired ox came in from the fields, hungry and ready to eat the hay. But the dog woke up, stood in the hay, and barked fiercely at the ox. He snapped his teeth and would not let the ox come near. "You selfish beast," said the ox. "You cannot eat the hay yourself, and yet you will not let those eat who are truly hungry."
Do not keep others from enjoying what you cannot enjoy yourself.

THE BAT, THE BIRDS, AND THE BEASTS

A great war broke out between the birds and the beasts of the forest. The bat did not know which side to join. When the birds were winning, the bat flew with them, saying, "I have wings, so I am a bird." But the next day, the beasts were winning. The bat quickly went to them, saying, "I have teeth and fur, so I am a beast." When the war was over, the birds and beasts made peace. But neither side would accept the bat, because he had been loyal to no one. He was chased away to live alone in the dark.
Those who have no true loyalty have no true friends.
